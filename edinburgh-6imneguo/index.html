Two days in Edinburgh

Day 1: Castle, illusions and ghosts

Breakfast at the hotel around 8. Then it's mostly downhill through the Old Town, with a ghost tour into the underground vaults, burgers for dinner, and the city lights from Calton Hill.

      Good to know

      Meal and snack times: the plan is built around lunch at 12:30, dinner at 5, and snacks mid-morning and mid-afternoon. Carry a bag of dairy-free snacks each day. Attraction cafés are unreliable for allergens, and the snack stops fall between sights rather than at cafés.

      Dairy-free: ask for the allergen menu everywhere. Burger buns, batter and puff pastry are the usual hidden sources of dairy, and chips are normally safe. A Greggs vegan sausage roll is a reliable dairy-free sausage roll. Ask for allergen info at attraction cafés too, including attraction cafés. Edinburgh has a strong vegan scene, so Google Maps "vegan" searches near the Old Town will turn up backup options.

      Book before you go: the Castle (first slot), the kids' ghost tour, Real Mary King's Close, and both dinners, since 5pm tables are easy to get but worth securing. Check the ghost tour's times first and fit the afternoon around it.

      Let them choose: give each child one pick from the extra ideas. Being in charge of part of the weekend makes kids far more invested than any single attraction.

      Going near Halloween? Edinburgh goes big on it. The Samhuinn Fire Festival is usually on 31 October, and the spooky stops on this plan suit that week perfectly.

      Shoes: nothing on the plan is muddy, but the Old Town is steep and cobbled, so wear comfortable trainers.

      If it rains: spend longer at Dynamic Earth or go back to the National Museum, which is too big for one visit anyway. Calton Hill is the only stop really worth skipping in bad weather. The ghost tour runs in the rain, so bring jackets.

      Opening times: check on the day. The National Museum closes at 5pm. Restaurants change too, so it's worth a quick look before relying on one.
